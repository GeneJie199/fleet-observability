# Contributor and provenance policy

The complete audited histories identify GeneJie and GeneJie199 identities; existing
copyright notices name GeneJie199. No ownership transfer to a company is inferred.
Historical Apache grants remain effective. Undisclosed employer/contract restrictions
cannot be determined from Git metadata and must be escalated if discovered.

DCO is useful provenance certification, but its text certifies submission under the
indicated open source license; it is not a general independent commercial relicensing
grant. Because future dual licensing/OEM is intended, use a reviewed contributor license
agreement for new third-party copyrightable contributions before merge. DCO sign-off
may supplement it, not silently replace it. Trivial corrections can receive a recorded
maintainer exception after rights review; exceptions must not imply commercial rights
that were never obtained.

CLA direction: contributor retains copyright; grants the named project rights holder
nonexclusive, worldwide rights to reproduce, modify, distribute, sublicense and offer
the contribution under Community and commercial terms, with an appropriate patent
grant and authority/employer representation. Incorporate corporate consent where
necessary. Do not treat opening a PR as signing an unreviewed CLA. No agreement is
automatically signed by this change; acceptance tooling is not yet implemented.

Use established individual/corporate CLA practice as a drafting reference, with a
project-specific reviewed agreement before accepting external code for dual licensing.
Legal review recommended before broad commercial operation.

References: <https://developercertificate.org/> and
<https://www.apache.org/licenses/contributor-agreements.html>.
